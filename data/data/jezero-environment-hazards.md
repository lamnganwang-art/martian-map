# Jezero Crater Environment and Astronaut Hazards

## 1. Radiation and space weather
- Hazard: Radiation from space and solar activity can increase health risks for astronauts.
- Why it matters: Crews need shielding, limited exposure time, and awareness of solar activity.
- Data source: NASA MAVEN space-weather context and MEDA surface-radiation measurements.
- Map display: Show a general “radiation awareness” layer, not an exact personal dose prediction.
- Planning response: If elevated space-weather activity is indicated, reduce EVA duration or delay the Marswalk.

## 2. Dust
- Hazard: Fine Martian dust can reduce visibility, stick to equipment, and affect solar power.
- Why it matters: Dust can interfere with instruments, moving parts, solar panels, and habitat systems.
- Data source: NASA MEDA dust and weather measurements.
- Map display: Show current or recent dust conditions and mark periods of poor visibility.
- Planning response: Avoid Marswalks during high-dust conditions; protect cameras and equipment.

## 3. Temperature extremes
- Hazard: Mars has large temperature changes between day and night.
- Why it matters: Astronauts need thermal protection, and equipment may perform differently in extreme cold or heat.
- Data source: NASA MEDA air and ground temperature measurements.
- Map display: Show representative day and night temperature ranges.
- Planning response: Schedule Marswalks during favorable daylight and temperature conditions.

## 4. Wind
- Hazard: Wind can move dust and affect equipment.
- Why it matters: Wind can increase dust exposure and complicate operations.
- Data source: NASA MEDA wind-speed and wind-direction measurements.
- Map display: Show wind direction and speed context.
- Planning response: Consider wind direction when planning dust exposure and equipment placement.

## 5. Low atmospheric pressure
- Hazard: Mars has a very thin atmosphere and low surface pressure.
- Why it matters: Astronauts require pressure suits and life-support systems outside a habitat.
- Data source: NASA MEDA pressure measurements.
- Map display: Show pressure as background environmental context.
- Planning response: Treat every outside activity as requiring a pressure suit and life-support planning.

## 6. Terrain hazards
- Hazard: Steep slopes, rocky areas, crater walls, and rough terrain.
- Why it matters: These can make travel difficult and increase the risk of falls or equipment damage.
- Data source: USGS/NASA elevation and imagery products.
- Map display: Mark caution and avoid zones.
- Planning response: Choose routes that avoid the steepest or roughest areas where possible.
